PAD-5 with CDC Expansion Board

Description

The Model PAD-5-CDC Expansion Board is a PAD-5 plug in board that allows conventional detectors to be connected to the Fire Alarm system. It provides four conventional detection zones that can be wired Class A or Class B. Each zone supports two-wire photoelectric or ionization compatible smoke detectors and an unlimited number of normally open circuit closure devices. The Siemens Fire Safety flame and beam detectors are also supported. Relay bases, remote lamps, and audible bases may also be used with the PAD-5-CDC. The PAD-5-CDC uses one address on the P2 loop. The PAD-5-CDC address is always one more than the PAD-5 main board that it is plugged into. The DPU is used to program the P2 address of the PAD-5-CDC. There are LED indicators for each zone, as well as for the P2 interface and for the status of the board. The PAD-5 Main Board is capable of supporting a single expansion board (PAD-5-CDC or PAD-5-CLSA).

Each of the four programmable zones can be configured for the type of detectors installed on the zone or can be disabled if not used. Detectors requiring 100mA zone current limit may not be installed on zones that are configured for the 175mA current limit. The current is automatically set by the Zeus programming tool, based on the detector model selected. Each loop is protected against short circuits and transient voltages as required.

  • Do not use shorting devices on circuits with accessories on them. The shorting devices will lower the zone voltage to a point where the accessories will not operate.
  • Any or all of the circuits may be programmed to operate with Alarm Verification. This allows the automatic verification of alarm conditions caused by smoke detectors in order to prevent nuisance alarms.
  • Shorting devices may be used on zones that are configured for Alarm Verification. If a shorting device on an Alarm Verified zone is activated, the zone will immediately go into alarm, bypassing the Alarm Verification sequence.

Mounting the Board

  1. Remove the daughter bypass board that is factory installed on X901 (location shown in Figure 2), before attaching the PAD-5-CDC board to the PAD-5-MB.
  2. Remove the mounting screw (screw “A”) next to expansion connector X900 (do not discard) the screw will be reused.
  3. Attach the two 13/16” standoffs provided, unto the backbox as shown on Figure 2.
  4. Attach the male-female standoff to the PAD-5-MB as shown in Figure 2.
  5. Plug in the board as shown in Figure 2. Align the two connectors from the PAD-5-CDC with the two connectors on the main board.
  6. Use the two 13/16” screws provided to secure the board in place.
  7. Attach screw “A” to the PAD-5-CDC board and the male-female standoff as shown in Figure 2.
CAUTION

It is recommended that the printed circuit boards be removed for any procedure that may cause dust, metal shavings, grease, or any such matter that may affect the circuit boards and/or parts. All field wiring and connections to the PAD-5-MB must be disconnected, and AC must be disconnected before removing fasteners and removing the board assembly from the enclosure.

Wire Routing

All high voltage and non-power limited wiring must be kept separate from power limited wir-ing. A ¼” separation must be maintained, with high voltage and non-power limited wiring running in separate conduit openings from power wiring.

To avoid induced noise (transfer of electrical energy from one wire to another), keep input wiring isolated from high current output and power-limited wiring. Improper wiring installation may cause improper operation. Avoid pulling one multi-conductor cable for the entire system. Instead, separate high current input/output from low current.

Wiring within the cabinet should be routed around the perimeter of the cabinet. It should not cross the printed circuit board where it could induce noise into the sensitive microelectronics or pick up unwanted RF noise from the switching power supply circuit.

Wiring Instructions

Remove all system power before installation. First remove battery power, and then AC. To power up, connect AC first and then battery.

To Connect the External Wiring:

  1. Loosen the screw of the terminal by turning it counterclockwise.
  2. Insert the wire into the side of the terminal block.
  3. Tighten the screw of the terminal block by turning it clockwise.

Each detector or group of detectors must use a two-wire circuit of 12 to 18 AWG, thermoplastic fixture wire. If permitted by local building codes. The PAD-5-CDC may be connected to existing device loops that have a 3.9K or 4.7K end-of-line resistor (1/4W, 5%) or a 50uF, 50V end-of-line capacitor installed. Always use a 50uF, 50V end-of-line capacitor for new installations and whenever practical in existing detector loops for all detector models except the Siemens PBA-1191, which requires a 4.7K end-of-line resistor, as shown in Figure 3. Each detector zone can be wired Class A or Class B individually.

The screw terminals can accommodate one 12 -18 AWG.

Wiring Notes

Refer to the views below:

  1. All circuits can be Class A or Class B.
  2. All circuits are power limited to NFPA 70 per NEC 760.
  3. All circuits are supervised.
  4. Refer to UL Compatible Two-Wire Detector List for the maximum number of detec-tors per zone. See Section 4 Compatible Detectors for details.
  5. Do not mix smoke detectors with the PBA-1191.
  6. Do not mix different manufacturer’s smoke detectors on the same zone.
  7. When mixing different model detectors on a zone, do not install more detectors than the smallest number shown in the “Number of Detectors per Zone” column of the compatibility table. For example, if installing Siemens PE-11 and PE-3 detectors on the same zone, do not install more than fifteen detectors on that zone. When con-figuring the zone in Zeus, enter the model name of the detector with the smallest number (PE-3 in this example).
  8. Max resistance: 70 ohms/circuit. 40 ohms if accessories (relay/audible bases) are used.
  9. Do not use shorting devices on circuits with accessories on them.
  10. Only one device accessory per circuit is guaranteed to activate on the PAD-5-CDC.
  11. Positive and negative ground fault detected at <1K Ohms for zone terminals.

Zone Circuits

The PAD-5-CDC has four zone Circuits. Each zone circuit has a corresponding connector at the top of the circuit board. For example, Zone 1 corresponds to X211, Zone 2 corresponds to X212, Zone 3 corresponds to X213 and Zone 4 Corresponds to X214.

FDNet (P2) Wiring

The P2 Interface is the connection between the fire control panel and the PAD-5 Main Board. The connection is made through the main board external wiring to X202, which is plugged into the main board. See the PAD-5-MB Installation Instructions, Document ID A6V101030358, for wiring information.

Device Programming Unit (DPU) Connection

Terminals on the board are present for the Device Programming Unit (DPU). The P2 address for the board must be set by the DPU.

WARNING

P2 address cannot be programmed while the board is mounted.
