About the Model XNLC

The Model XNLC Intelligent Notification Loop Card from Siemens Industry, Inc., is the interface for providing an enhanced high power line connection between the Desigo Fire Safety Modular/Cerberus PRO Modular System and ACEND™ line of Intelligent Notification Appliances. The application program that is loaded into the on-board microprocessor controls the XNLC operation.

The XNLC contains 16 LEDs for diagnosis of problems and monitors for ground fault detection on the loop. All field appliances connected to the XNLC are addressed and tested using the Device Programming Unit (DPU).

Operation

The XNLC initializes, operates, and maintains all appliances residing on the loop. The XNLC has two 1.5A Line Driver circuits for a total card capacity of 3A. Each Line Driver circuit will provide power to 2 Class A/X loops or 4 Class B stubs, supporting a maximum of 126 Intelligent notification appliances per line driver, with a total of 252 appliances supported per card. The PMI tells the XNLC which notification appliances on its lines should indicate an alarm condition when there is an alarm. The XNLC powers, communicates with, monitors and controls all notification appliances on the high-power loops/stubs.

Note: PMI = PMI-2/PMI-3/FCM2041-U2/U3

The XNLC supports four addressable Class A (DCLA) or Class X (DCLC) loops or eight Class B (DCLB) parallel lines/stubs of up to 252 Intelligent notification appliances (i.e. Horns, Horn-Strobes, Speakers, Low Frequency Sounder-Strobes (LFSS), etc.) in any combination. The notification appliances have built-in isolators that will isolate any notification appliance from the short if the line is shorted (on a Class X loop only).

Class X (DCLC) wiring is supported under the ULC-S527-19 4th Edition, Standard for Control Units for Fire Alarm Systems.

Controls and Indicators

The front panel of the XNLC contains one reset switch, 16 LEDs, and three HNET address switches, as shown in Figure 1. A reset switch is located on the top of the front panel. Pushing the reset switch re-initializes the XNLC operation.

The LEDs are located below the reset switch, and their functions are defined as follows:

POWER

 

(Green)

Normally ON. When illuminated, it indicates the power for the XNLC is applied to the card. Blinks during firmware download or if download has failed.

CARD FAIL

(Yellow)

Normally OFF. When illuminated, it indicates the card microprocessor has failed.

HNET

(Yellow)

Normally OFF. When illuminated, it indicates that the HNET communication with the XNLC has terminated due to an HNET failure.

GND FAULT

 

(Yellow)

Normally OFF. When illuminated, it indicates that the XNLC has detected either a negative or positive ground fault on its field wiring. Blinks during firmware download.

ALARM

(Red)

Always OFF.

TROUBLE

(Yellow)

Normally OFF. When illuminated, it indicates that the XNLC has detected a trouble on its field wiring; or an incompatibility between firmware in either the PMI, XNLC, or both.

OPEN

 

(Yellow)

Normally OFF. When illuminated, it indicates that the XNLC has detected an open on its field wiring.

SHORT

(Yellow)

Normally OFF. When illuminated, it indicates that the XNLC has detected a short on its field wiring.

LINE LEDS 1 - 8

(Yellow)

Normally OFF. When illuminated, it indicates a trouble (open or short) on associated line or loop.

Pre-Installation

Three rotary dial switches at the bottom of the front panel are used to set the HNET network address of the XNLC. All field appliances connected to the XNLC are addressed and tested using the Device Programming Unit (DPU). Refer to the DPU Manual, P/N 315-033260, for further information.

Set the three-digit HNET network address for the XNLC using the three rotary dial switches located near the bottom of the front panel. (Refer to Figure 1 for the location of the switches.) The address for the XNLC must be the same as the address selected for it in the Zeus Programming Tool. To set the address, turn the pointers on each of the three dials to the numbers for the selected address. For example, if the address is 123, set the pointer for the HUNDREDS dial to “1”, set the pointer for the TENS dial to “2”, and set the pointer for the ONES dial to “3”. The range of allowable addresses is from 001 to 251 (leading zeros must be used).

USB Connector

The USB connector provides an interface to download new firmware to the XNLC. A standard USB A-B cable connects to a PC running the Zeus Programming Tool. Zeus is used to select the new firmware and initiate the download to the XNLC. Both the POWER LED and the GND FAULT LED will blink during download. The POWER LED will blink alone if the firmware is not completely downloaded to the XNLC.

Installation

WARNING

Remove all system power before installation; first battery, then AC. To power up, connect the AC first, then the battery.

The XNLC plugs perpendicularly into one slot in the CC-5/CC-2 card-cage via two 96-pin DIN connectors and can occupy any slot in the card-cage.

  1. Insert the XNLC Line card face upward into the card guides.
  2. Slide the card in until the card's edge connectors contact the receptacles on the motherboard.
  3. Verify that the DIN connectors of the card and the card-cage are aligned properly. The card can only be plugged into the card-cage in one direction; if it does not align, DO NOT FORCE the card.
  4. Place thumbs on the front panel adjacent to the captive screws and gently apply even pressure on the card until the connectors seat in the receptacles on the motherboard.
  5. Secure the card with captive screws.

Wiring

Refer to Appendix A for wiring topologies supported by the XNLC.

All field wiring to the XNLC is connected to the terminal blocks of the CC-5/CC-2 card cage slot in which it is installed.

NOTICE

All Intelligent Notification Appliances must be wired in Isolator Mode.

To Connect External Wiring

  1. Loosen the screw of the terminal by turning it counterclockwise.
  2. Insert the wire into the side of the terminal block.
  3. Tighten the screw of the terminal block by turning it clockwise.

Connect +24V from the PSC-12 or PSC-12M to terminals 17 and 19. Connect the -24V from the PSC-12 or PSC-12M to terminal 18 and 20 of the slot in the CC-5/CC-2 where the XNLC will be installed. (Refer to Figure 3.)

NOTES:

  1. No end of line device required.
  2. All circuits are supervised and power limited per NEC 760 and NFPA 70.
  3. Positive or negative ground fault detected at < 10K Ohms on terminal 1-16. Shorts are detected at 0.1 Ohms or less. Opens are detected at 800 ohms.

The XNLC circuit can be wired Class A (DCLA) or Class X (DCLC) loops or eight Class B (DCLB). The configuration tool allows selection of Class A, X or B. Refer to the Wiring Compatibility Guide for which circuits can be mixed in the same conduit with the XNLC. Electrical connections for the zones are shown in Figure 4. The wiring style is set in the Zeus Programming Tool.

If the return wires in a zone are reversed (in a Class A (DCLA) or Class X (DCLC) configuration), the line numbers and short LEDs associated with the affected zone will be illuminated. Switch the two return wires to remove this trouble. Eliminate all other troubles before completing the installation.

Compatibility

The XNLC supports compatible appliances with the model numbers listed in Table 1 for UL-864, UL-2572, ULC-S527, and ULC-S576. Use any combination of those listed in Table 1 as applicable.

  1. The XNLC has two separate Line Driver circuits that can be wired in isolation mode, distributed across four Class A (DCLA) or Class X (DCLC) loops, or eight Class B (DCLB) lines/stubs. A mix of Class A (DCLA)/ Class X (DCLC) loops and Class B (DCLB) lines/stubs are supported.
  2. All circuits are power limited to NFPA 70 per NEC 760. If permitted by local building codes, the XNLC supports the use of 12-22 AWG non-shielded, twisted, thermoplastic fixture wire without conduit.
  3. No end of line termination is required.
  4. Total circuit resistance of each loop/stub must not exceed 50 Ohms. Refer to the graph displayed in Figure 5 below, displaying the maximum wire and isolator resistance allowed based on the total appliance current for each loop/stub. Refer to Table 2 for wire resistance.
  5. Maximum capacitance: 0.5μF between line+ and line- 1.0μF between line and chassis per line driver.
  6. T-tapping is not allowed on addressable Class A (DCLA) or Class X (DCLC) loops.
  7. All models are UL and ULC listed.
  8. The intelligent notification appliances are programmed using a DPU.

For more information on specific candela and horn settings, refer to the individual notification appliance's installation instructions in Table 1. The instructions can be accessed via a document id search at https://sid.siemens.com/home.

NOTICE

Each appliance contains an isolator which adds 0.024 Ohms per appliance.

To determine the maximum wire and isolator resistance on a given loop/stub, refer to the following steps:

  1. Determine the current draw for each appliance found in the ACEND appliances' installation instructions and multiply it by the total number of each appliance type.
  2. Add up the appliance currents for each loop/stub and find this value on the X axis.
  3. Multiply the isolator resistance (0.024 Ohms) by the number of appliances required and subtract this value (total isolator resistance) from the total resistance. This will give you the total wire resistance.
  4. To find the total resistance allowed for the loop/stub, trace up to the curve and read across to the Y axis. This is the resistance for all wires and isolators in the loop/stub.

To determine maximum wire length required, refer to the following steps:

  1. Divide the total wire resistance by the wire's resistance based on its AWG found in Table 2.
  2. Multiply that number by 1000 to determine the total wire length required.

The total appliance current cannot exceed 1.5A per driver. Each driver supports up to four Class B stubs or two Class A/X loops. If the total current on the first driver exceeds 1.5A, move appliances to the second driver on the XNLC or add additional XNLC cards if both drivers are loaded to 1.5A.

Example scenario

A loop has 15 Horn/Strobes set to 15cd and 15 Horn/Strobes set to 30cd. From the ACEND appliance installation instructions each 15cd Horn (steady-high) /strobe draws 32m mA and each 30cd Horn (steady-high) /strobe draws 36mA. The total appliance current on the loop is 15x32mA + 15x36mA = 1.02A. Reading from the graph in Figure 5, the total resistance (wire and isolator resistance) is 15 Ohms. From these 15 Ohms, we must subtract the resistance due to the isolators in each appliance. For 30 appliances we have 30x0.024 = 0.72 Ohms. Subtracting the isolator resistance from the 15 Ohms of total resistance gives 15-0.72 = 14.28 Ohms of wire resistance. This is the total resistance for all wires in the loop or stub. For a Class B stub using 16AWG wire, the total wire length is 14.28 Ohms /4.89 Ohms (per 1000ft.) = 2920 feet of wire, or a stub length of 1460 feet.
